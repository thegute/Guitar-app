# KGuitar Theory Course: plan (2026-11-11, waiting for the owner's OK)

Replaces the old Offtonic-based theory lessons entirely (good videos/exercises from it are reused). One course for guitar,
ukulele and baritone (neck pictures, exercises and string talk follow the instrument). Lessons are all open with a suggested
path (Continue); every level ends with a Mastery check that is strongly suggested but never required.

## What the research showed
- **No Bull Music Theory for Guitarists** calls itself "the essential knowledge all guitarists need" and covers "the absolute
  basics" in 12 bite-size chapters (alphabet → minor keys) plus a barre-chord appendix. It is beginner to early intermediate,
  so it is the spine of Levels 1–3, not the whole course. Its method (go in order, quiz after each chapter, practical neck
  exercises, re-read, find it in songs, make your own) is the method of every lesson.
- **JustinGuitar Practical Music Theory** (grades 1–7): notes/sharps/octaves → neck notes, TAB, diagrams → major scale + key
  signatures → triads → chords in keys + progressions → fretboard notes + cycle of 5ths → intervals → 4-note chords →
  pentatonics + minors → harmonic analysis → extensions → CAGED → modes.
- **Pickup Music** (beginner → late intermediate pathways): theory always applied the same week; maps in this order: intervals
  across the neck → 3:2 pentatonic → CAGED → triads → inversions → 3-notes-per-string → arpeggios / chord-tone targeting;
  rhythm has its own grades (grid, subdivision, syncopation, feel); every grade ends with a quiz and a play-along piece.
- **Berklee Music Theory Fundamentals**: rudiments → rhythm → major scale → intervals → triads → natural minor → minor-key
  triads → song form → voice leading and inversions. **Musicians Institute Harmony & Theory 1**: major scales + key
  signatures → intervals and triads → circle of 4ths → diatonic and altered intervals.
- **Tom Kolb, Music Theory for Guitarists** (his book; GIT teacher) runs fretboard → basics → scales + keys → intervals →
  triads → harmonizing major → chord construction → harmonizing minor → key centers + modulation + modal interchange →
  blues + pentatonics → modes → other scales + arpeggios → substitution + reharmonization: the outline for Levels 5–8.
- Shared pattern: start with the notes on the strings; one idea per lesson; hear it, see it on the neck, play it, use it in
  music the same day; rhythm threaded through; maps of the neck build on each other; a check at the end of each level.

## Ear training, woven through (owner 2026-11-11: "so important", tied to what the lesson teaches)
Research: music schools treat ear training as a core skill next to harmony (Berklee runs Ear Training 1–4 alongside Harmony 1–4;
its Ear Training 1 grows in step with the theory: 2nds → 3rds and the tonic triad → tendency tones and I–IV–V → 4ths/5ths and
minor triads → 6ths, 7ths, diatonic triads → cycle-of-fifths patterns, always with rhythm). Hearing notes by their ROLE in a key
(scale degrees over a drone or cadence, "functional" hearing) carries over to real music far better than naming intervals out of
context; song-based interval tags help early but only cover set degrees. So: functional hearing is the backbone, intervals and
chord qualities are learned in the same lesson as their theory, and every ear drill ends with playing what you heard (the mic
checks it). KGuitar already has the pieces: the drone Ear trainer, ear quiz modes, the Rhythm trainer, Find the chords, the
Riff Scanner, Jam backing.

Every lesson's "Hear it" step drills exactly what it taught; a daily 3-minute ear warm-up follows the current lesson; spaced
review brings ear items back; every Mastery check has an ear part.
- L1 Notes on the neck: higher or lower? · match a note (sing or play it back) · unisons and octaves (with octave shapes) ·
  tuning by ear (the beats) · echo a rhythm.
- L2 Scales, chords, keys: do-re-mi = degrees 1–2–3 over a drone · major vs minor triad · each interval with its lesson
  (2nds, 3rds, 4ths/5ths, 6ths, 7ths) · flat intervals (b3, b7) · "where is home?" · I, IV and V by sound · write down a rhythm.
- L3 Progressions, pentatonics, minor: I–IV–V–vi by ear · find the key of a song · the V→I pull · call and response with
  pentatonic licks · b3, b6, b7 over a minor drone · major or minor key? · follow the 12-bar changes by ear · straight or swing?
- L4 Fretboard: hear a phrase, play it in two places · which triad inversion (top note)? · 3rds and 6ths by sound · 16ths.
- L5 Chords: the five 7th-chord sounds · sus vs major vs minor · hear the colour of 9, 11, 13 · altered tension · follow the
  moving voice in a voice-led change.
- L6 Soloing: chord tone or tension? · sing then play · transcribe short licks (Riff Scanner checks you) · answer the Jam band.
- L7 Harmony: cadence types · spot the secondary dominant · borrowed chords (iv, bVII) · hear a key change · work out a real
  song's chords by ear.
- L8 Modes: each mode's colour note over a drone (Dorian 6, Mixolydian b7, Lydian #4, Phrygian b2, Locrian b5) · harmonic minor
  sound · altered colours · triad pairs.

## How every lesson works (UX)
1 **Learn** (deep: why it matters, the idea from the ground up, worked examples, common mistakes) · 2 **Hear it** (ear
training for exactly this idea) · 3 **Watch** (the best video for this spot) · 3 **Play** (exercises
that open in Play; the mic follows you) · 4 **Use it** (a song, Jam backing, a tool) · 5 **Check** (a short quiz) ·
6 **In your books** (the pages in his own books on the same thing, from the private book pack).
Level page: progress ring, lessons with ticks, "Continue", Mastery check (stars; test out of a level you know).

## The levels (books that feed each one in brackets)
**Level 1 · Notes on the neck** [No Bull 1–2, Kolb 1–2, Fretboard Mastery, Fretboard Workbook, Fretboard Memorize]
1 The strings and the musical alphabet · 2 Tones, semitones, sharps and flats · 3 The notes on the low E and A strings
(landmark frets) · 4 Octave shapes: every note on every string · 5 Reading tab, chord boxes and neck diagrams ·
6 Rhythm 1: the beat, quarters and eighths · 7 How the strings are tuned (4ths and the G–B 3rd) · Mastery check

**Level 2 · Scales, chords and keys** [No Bull 3–8, Kolb 3–6, Circle of Fifths]
1 The major scale · 2 Major and minor triads (open and barre shapes) · 3 Intervals from the major scale ·
4 Flat and sharp intervals (and chord names) · 5 Keys: home, family, parent scale · 6 Building the chords of any key ·
7 Key signatures and the circle of fifths · 8 Rhythm 2: strumming eighths, rests and ties · Mastery check

**Level 3 · Progressions, pentatonics and minor keys** [No Bull 9–12, Kolb 8–10]
1 Chord progressions as numbers · 2 Hearing the key and the V→I pull · 3 Minor pentatonic and the blues scale ·
4 Major pentatonic and the relative trick · 5 The natural minor scale · 6 Minor keys · 7 The 12-bar blues ·
8 Rhythm 3: triplets, shuffle and swing · Mastery check (= all of No Bull)

**Level 4 · Mapping the fretboard** [CAGED + 100 Licks, Fretboard Logic, Triads & Inversions, Guided Scale Practice]
1 The five pentatonic positions · 2 CAGED: one chord, five shapes · 3 The major scale in five positions ·
4 Triads on the top three strings (inversions) · 5 Triads on every string set · 6 Interval shapes everywhere (3rds, 6ths,
double stops) · 7 Three-notes-per-string scales · 8 Rhythm 4: sixteenths and syncopation · Mastery check

**Level 5 · Chords in depth** [Kolb 5–7, Triads for the Improvising Guitarist]
1 Augmented and diminished triads · 2 Seventh chords · 3 The 7th chords of a key · 4 Sus, add, 6 and power chords ·
5 Extensions: 9, 11, 13 · 6 Altered dominants · 7 Slash chords and bass lines · 8 Voice leading · Mastery check

**Level 6 · Chord tones, melody and soloing** [Improvise for Real, CAGED + 100 Licks, Dominant Pentatonic]
1 Arpeggios in CAGED shapes · 2 Seventh arpeggios · 3 Targeting chord tones on the changes · 4 Mixing major and minor
pentatonic · 5 Passing, approach and chromatic notes · 6 Phrasing: motifs, call and response, tension and release ·
7 Double stops and 6ths · 8 Playing what you hear · Mastery check

**Level 7 · Harmony in depth** [Kolb 8–9 and 13, Harmony in Practice]
1 Cadences · 2 Harmonic and melodic minor in minor keys · 3 Secondary dominants · 4 Borrowed chords (modal interchange) ·
5 Key centers and key changes · 6 Diatonic substitution and reharmonization · 7 Tritone subs and passing diminished ·
8 Analysing real songs · Mastery check

**Level 8 · Modes and beyond** [Practical Guide to Modes, Kolb 11–12, Triads for the Improvising Guitarist]
1 What modes really are · 2 Dorian and Mixolydian · 3 Lydian, Phrygian, Locrian · 4 Modal vamps and their colour chords ·
5 Harmonic minor and Phrygian dominant · 6 Melodic minor, Lydian dominant and altered · 7 Whole-tone and diminished ·
8 What to play over any chord · 9 Triad pairs and upper structures · Final mastery check

## Lesson standard (owner, after the sample lesson 2026-11-11)
"More depth, same or a tiny bit less quizzes." So: rich Learn steps (the why, the physics/logic behind it, landmarks, how it
connects to chords and later levels, two routes to every answer), 5–6 worked examples with step-by-step reasoning (not
quizzes), and at most ~5 quiz links across the practice week + 3 checks (Apply, Hear, Play) + Explain it back. Prefer playing
exercises in Play over extra quizzes. The sample (src/course/level1.ts, octave shapes) is the model for every lesson.
